# Dr Fable review: lesson loop, 2026-09-29 (branch feat/vs-battle-stage1-data, HEAD 2ebea64)

Read-only. Everything below was recomputed from `rollouts/qwen_lessons` + `rollouts/locked/lesson_loop_v1` (91 finished runs: 89 of 30 rounds, 2 two-round smokes) with the repo's own scripts and my own scripts. `pytest`: 396 passed, 2 skipped.

## Verdict: PARTLY on track

Ken is solved by the loop; Ryu is a weak, seed-dependent gain; Honda is not a lesson problem and no prompt variant has moved it. The documented numbers are honest and reproduce exactly, but two of the doc's framings overstate what was shown (per-opponent CIs, the chance baseline), and the last addition (the track record) silently switched the loop off against Honda.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Headline tables reproduce; the per-opponent intervals are too narrow (unit of analysis)
`compare_prompts.py` reproduces 0h and 0i exactly (views/character 18 seeds: Ken +29.3/+55.8, Ryu +24.5/+20.4, Honda -0.8/-14.9; fgc 12 seeds: +55.7/+33.5/-13.6; round 4 with/without track: Ken +56.1/+47.8, Ryu +0.4/-0.2, Honda +0.6/+1.1; pooled all NOT SHOWN). `track_record.py` rebuilds `lessons/track_record.json` byte-identical. `laya_evidence.py` reproduces 0g's numbers.

But the per-opponent CIs in `sf2/eval/stats.py:ci` (`compare_prompts.py:98`) treat 30 rounds of one run as independent, while every round of a run shares the same lessons and Qwen's luck. Between-run SD of the per-run mean is 12-25 hp. Re-done with the RUN as the unit (normal CI over per-run means):

| opp | views | character | character_fgc | fgc+track |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ken | +24.6 [+15.4,+33.7] n=15 | +55.8 [+49.8,+61.8] n=6 | +55.9 [+46.3,+65.5] n=8 | +47.8 [+31.3,+64.3] n=4 |
| Ryu | +19.6 [+4.3,+34.9] n=10 | +20.4 [+4.4,+36.3] n=6 | +17.0 [+2.6,+31.4] n=8 | -0.2 [-20.6,+20.1] n=4 |
| Honda | +0.7 [-6.9,+8.4] n=10 | -14.9 [-27.5,-2.2] n=6 | **-6.5 [-15.3,+2.4] n=8** | +1.1 [-5.5,+7.7] n=4 |

So: Ken helps under every prompt (robust to the 8 variants tried); the character prompt's Ken gain over views is real (+26 hp, non-overlapping run-level intervals); Ryu helps but only with 8+ seeds; **0h's "character_fgc hurts Honda -13.6 [-23.4,-3.7]" is not supported at the run level** (round 4 already showed it flip to +0.6). `track_record.py` already uses the run as unit; `compare_prompts.py` and `verdict.json` should too.

### 2. "Qwen claims hold 50-60% vs chance 9%" is not a fair baseline
Qwen is shown the verifier's own classes ("works / fails / unclear" per row, `character_prompt.py:_answers`, `VERDICT`; `lesson_prompt.py:COMPARE`), so proposing is largely copying a labelled row. Evidence: of 1,258 non-refused claims, **493 (39%) were registered at proposal** (already "clearly better/worse" on the data Qwen was looking at, `lessons.py:_judge`); 363 went to test, of which only a minority became registered (final: use_more 197 registered vs 173 rejected + 32 retired; always 8 registered vs 58 rejected). The doc's chance baseline (`qwen_lessons.py:random_claims`) draws uniformly over every move x range x when, mostly empty cells that hold by definition never ("few"). A random kind over populated cells only (>= 20 tries both sides) holds 16-27% (Ken 0.16, Ryu 0.23, Honda 0.27 on seed 21001, vs Qwen 0.42/0.73/0.40). Qwen is ~2x a table-aware baseline, not 5-6x, and much of that is reading labels. Also 672 of 1,930 proposals (35%) were refused, 543 of them as "already registered / rejected / testing": Qwen repeats itself despite being shown the registry.

### 3. Within-run learning exists only against Ken
Paired hp vs none, games 5-9 minus games 0-4, run as unit: **Ken +25.5 [+14.4,+36.7] (n=33)**; Ryu +3.5 [-16.0,+23.0]; Honda -7.2 [-17.5,+3.1]. The none arm drifts < 7 hp, so this is the lessons. Registered lessons do accumulate (Ken 1.2 -> 4.1, Ryu 1.6 -> 7.4, Honda 1.8 -> 4.4 by game 9), but against Ryu 7 registered lessons buy nothing extra and against Honda they trend negative. Owner goal "keep the good, negate the bad game after game": achieved for Ken, not shown for Ryu, not for Honda.

### 4. The track record switched the loop off against Honda; the doc calls it "no effect"
Round 4 Honda with `--track`: registered at end 1, 2, 1, 0 (without: 7, 6, 6, 3); hold rate 0.11/0.14/0.17/0.10 (without 0.60/0.77/0.32/0.25). 30 refusals "its track record hurts". A loop arm with 0-2 lessons is nearly the none arm, hence +1.1. Ken also lost lessons (5,2,3,2 vs 4,5,4,6) and 8 hp. The mechanism is also unsound as an attribution device: the Honda walk-in lesson was **never in play alone** (0 rounds); its 8 co-lessons all show -5 to -27 in the same rounds (e.g. "avoid sweep at mid range when he jumps" 363 rounds, -11.5). `track_record.py` documents this caveat, then refuses on it anyway, and the doc already saw "avoid spinning_bird_kick" flip hurts -> unclear with more data. Recommend: show the record, do not refuse on it.

### 5. Honda is not a lesson-grammar problem
Rounds won of 15 per arm, every Honda run: loop 0-3, none 0-5. No prompt variant, no lesson set, no track record changes that. Two clues in the data: (a) `rule == "nothing_left"` fired 485 times, only vs Honda: the avoid lessons ruled out every shortlist move and forward, and text laya's pick then matches nothing (follows_rule 0.000); (b) see finding 7 on block ratings. The open item from 0f (a test is not stopped when rounds tank) is still open.

### 6. Statistics: multiple comparisons and seeds
Eight loop variants were tried in one day (0b one-view, 0c relative, 0c+exceptions, 0d what-if, 0d+moves fix, character, character_fgc, +track), most with 2-4 seeds per opponent. With between-run SD ~20 hp for Ryu, 4 seeds give a +/-20 interval: round 3 Ryu fgc +33.5 vs round 4 +0.4 is exactly that. Any prompt-vs-prompt claim needs >= 8 seeds per opponent, pre-registered, run as the unit. Pairing itself is sound (none arms byte-identical across prompts, verified by `compare_prompts.pairs`; rounds with no lines in play: 0, so no accidental unpaired rounds). Leak check: `sf2/eval/logs.py:TEST_DIRS` excludes every loop dir from play data; lock runs read the snapshot; run.json marks test=true. No leak found. Hygiene: the two 2-round smoke runs (134810_ken, 145756_ken) are in `track_record.build` and `laya_evidence` (91 runs) though `export()` excludes them.

### 7. The fine-tune question: no general text-laya problem; one general laya-vision defect, fixable without training
`laya_evidence.py` measures "compliance" = share of decisions that picked the named move, and reads soft 53% as a laya problem. It is the label rule (`advice.py:answers` rule 4: a soft lesson is dropped when laya-vision says "likely fails"), so compliance is not a checkpoint measure. The direct measure is already logged per decision: **`follows_rule`** (`advisor.py:choose`). Over all loop-arm decisions: soft 0.986 (n=14,289), hard 0.979 (1,740), vision 1.000, walk 0.999; per opponent 0.97-1.00. Text laya follows its training rule faithfully against all three. No text-laya fine-tune is indicated.

laya-vision: in the no-advice arms, when he attacks on the ground (13,512 decisions, 3 opponents), a block is in the top 3 62% of the time but is rated above "likely fails" **0 times**. That is general and opponent-agnostic, and it is a scale mismatch (block score = P(blocked) with a 21% base rate vs attack score = P(hit)), not a learned error. Cheapest fix: rate a block on its own scale (P(blocked) vs base rate, or expected net) in `advice.rating`/`shortlist`; then only "always" no longer needs to override it. What would settle a fine-tune: after that calibration change, an offline replay of the 13.5k rows showing blocks still mis-ranked against the logged net of attacks in the same situation, per opponent and pooled. Also cheap: replace laya_evidence's top-3 split with a split by rating word, and report follows_rule.

### 8. Loop complexity
The verifier (`lessons.py`, 213 lines) is small and correct (0 violations in 91 runs, tests green). The growth is in prompts and side mechanisms: three prompt modules, what-if, exceptions, primer, track record. `opp_move` is logged (`opp_moves.py`) but not yet in any view, although it is the owner's stated next goal ("if I see X").
